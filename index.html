What the optimiser is choosing

Before the blackout it decides how many large, medium and small generators to buy within budget. During it, it decides every hour which generators run and what share \(x_{s,t}\) of each department's demand gets covered. Fuel is finite, so every kilowatt-hour spent now is one that can't be spent later.

The objective weighs two goals that pull against each other:

\[ \max \; \lambda \sum_{t}\sum_{s} \frac{W_s\, x_{s,t}}{(1+\alpha)^{t-1}} \;+\; (1-\lambda)\, K\, z^{\min} \]

The first term rewards serving important departments, early. The second rewards the worst-served department, so nobody is cut off. The priority dial is \(\lambda\).

Things to try

  • Critical care runs at full power for twelve hours. Then the fuel is gone and the building goes dark.
  • Critical care holds on for all 48 hours at a fifth to a third of its demand, and the support departments get a trickle.
  • Three extra large generators buy a hybrid: full power for the first nine hours, then a steady ration until the grid comes back.
